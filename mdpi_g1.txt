Analysis of Vibration Characteristics of Cross-Type Hydraulic 
Pipeline Based on Finite Element Method

Abstract: Branched hydraulic lines are prone to leak age and break under complex working condi-
tions. The coupling vibration problem caused by the ﬂuid pressure beat and external mechanical 
excitation of the pump at the junction of the bran ch of the cross-type hydraulic pipeline is very 
common. So a method combining ﬁnite element simulation and ﬂuid-structure interaction vibration 
experiments is proposed for research. First, the possibility of the ﬁnite element method in solving 
the pipeline vibration problem is veri ﬁed, and then the modal analysis and harmonious response 
analysis of the cross-type pipe are carried out to discuss the in ﬂuence of ﬂuid pressure and solid 
elastic modulus on pipeline vibration. The results show that as the ﬂuid pressure and pipeline elastic 
modulus change, diﬀerent modes of diﬀerent orders will be aﬀected. Under external excitation, only 
a portion of the order is sensitive to external excitation. 
Keywords: branch pipeline; ﬂuid-structure interaction; vibration analysis; ﬁnite element simulation 
 
1. Introduction 
The vibration problem of pipeline systems has a broad engineering application back-
ground, and its research results can be directly applied to various ﬁelds such as ocean 
engineering, bioengineering, chemical machin ery, aerospace, and the nuclear industry. 
Whatʹs more, cross-type branch pipelines are widely used in hydraulic systems and pipe-
lines [1]. However, during the transportation of gases and liquids, intermittent suction or 
discharge movements of ﬂuids in pipelines may cause ﬂow and pressure pulsations in the 
pipeline system, leading to severe pipeline vibration in discontinuous areas, especially at 
branch points. At the interface between the branch pipeline and the main pipeline, the 
pressure ﬂow pulsation of hydraulic oil and the ﬂuid–structure interaction phenomenon 
caused by the external vibration of the pipeline are relatively concentrated. Abnormal or 
excessive vibration can lead to fatigue failure, leakage, and other safety issues in pipeline 
systems. 
Therefore, the problems surrounding the branch pipeline have aroused the research 
interest of many scholars. In recent years,  KARAGIOZIS et al. [2 ] of the United States 
analyzed the nonlinear stability of incompressible ﬂow in cylindrical thin shells, combin-
ing the linear model and the Donnell shallow shell nonlinear equation in the model, and 
discussed its impact on pipeline stability by changing the length diameter ratio and thick-
ness diameter ratio. In 2008, TIJSSELING A S [3] studied the plumbing composed of 
straight pipes and elbows. The theoretical mode l is based on the idea of Tentarelli, and 
the models of two pipe segments are establishe d. The straight pipe is solved in the fre-
quency domain, and the solution of the elbow is not divided into units. The elbow model 
is directly used for the overall solution, and the inﬂuence of the bending radius and angle 
of the elbow on the ﬂuid–solid coupling vibration characteristics is analyzed. Chao 
Jiaming [4] conducted airﬂow pulsation and pipeline vibration tests on the primary intake

on the ﬂuid–structure interaction and vibration mechanism around the junction between 
the branch pipe and the main pipe. Therefore, how to analyze the coupling phenomenon 
caused by the ﬂuid pressure pulsation of the pump and external mechanical excitation at 
the branch junction remains an important issue in pipeline dynamics analysis. 
Based on the above analysis, the ﬂuid–structure interaction vibration model of cross-
type branch pipe is established by using the ﬁnite element method. Considering the exci-
tation caused by ﬂuid pressure ﬂuctuations and external excitation, the fluid–structure 
interaction dynamics problem of the branch pipe is solved. In the article, the excitation 
caused by pressure pulsation is simulated through changes in ﬂuid pressure inside the 
pipe, and external excitation is simulated through the addition of simple harmonic force 
in harmonic response analysis. And the impact of pipeline materials and ﬂuid pressure 
on its vibration characteristics is analyzed. The accuracy of the ﬁnite element method in 
solving the fluid–structure interaction vibration problem of pipelines is veri ﬁed by com-
paring the ﬁnite element calculation results with the vibration experiment results.

The problems caused by the di ﬀerence between the experimental conditions of the 
conﬁrmatory experiment and the simulation cond itions are mainly due to the following 
reasons: (1) The installation position of th e sensor: During the measurement of natural 
frequency, the installation position of the sensor should avoid the position of the con-
cerned modal node. For example, in this experiment, the vibration amplitude at the mid-
span position of the pipeline is de ﬁnitely large, but the mid-span is a node with even 
modal orders, so it should be avoided to install the sensor at this position. (2) On the other 
hand, the modes of the structure belong to strong directional modes, and diﬀerent orders 
of modes are often in di ﬀerent directions. The installation direction of the sensor in this 
experiment has always been upward, which can easily cause errors. In the ﬁnite element 
simulation calculation process, only the inherent modal parameters of the model are cal-
culated, and the structure is not aﬀected by sensors, so there is a certain error between the 
two. 
Therefore, when conducting pipeline vibr ation experiments, the following opera-
tions should be carried out to reduce possible errors: (1) the installation position of the 
sensor should not be close to the modal no de position; (2) when measuring the natural 
frequencies in diﬀerent directions, sensors should be arranged in diﬀerent directions.

3. Fluid–Structure Interaction Analysis of Cross-Type Hydraulic Pipeline 
The coupling vibration of the branch pipeline is mainly caused by the ﬂuid pressure 
pulsation excitation of the pump and external  mechanical excitation. This section simu-
lates the excitation caused by pressure pulsation through changes in ﬂuid pressure inside 
the pipe. In the process of studying the vibration characteristics of hydraulic pipelines, the 
coupling eﬀect of ﬂuid on solid pipelines will obviously aﬀect the vibration characteristics 
of the pipeline, while the coupling eﬀect of solid on ﬂuid has little impact on them. There-
fore, this section only considers the ﬂuid–structure interaction eﬀect of ﬂuid on pipelines 
in one direction. The ﬂuid–structure interaction analysis ﬁrst uses the computational ﬂuid

type pipeline still adopts the straight pipe modeling method mentioned above, and after 
geometric modeling, the SOLSH190 element in  the continuum shell element is used to

3.1. Inﬂuence of Fluid Pressure on Fluid–Structure Interaction Characteristics of  
Cross-Type Pipeline 
In order to consider the coupled vibration of the pipeline caused by ﬂuid pressure 
pulsation, the ﬂuid pressure values are set as 0 MPa, 1 MPa, 5 MPa, 10 MPa, 15 MPa, and 
21 MPa, and the inﬂuence of ﬂuid pressure on it is discussed by solving the ﬂuid–structure 
interaction steady-state parameters under six working conditions. The treatment method 
for the ﬂuid is the same as that for using the ﬁnite element method for the single pipeline, 
with boundary conditions set. The inlet of the cross-type pipe is set as the velocity inlet, 
and the ﬂow rate is 1 m/s. The outlet is set as the pressure outlet. The pressure values 
correspond to the six working conditions, and the ﬂuid–solid interface is set as the wall. 
As the ﬂuid pressure increases, the maximum response value increases and the maximum 
stress decreases.

In passage [26], branch pipelines have b een calculated with the transfer matrix 
method. By comparing its calculation results with those of ﬁnite element calculation, it

branch pipelines. 
5. Conclusions 
In light of the coupling vibration problem caused by the ﬂuid pressure beat and ex-
ternal mechanical excitation of the pump at the junction of the branch of the cross-type 
hydraulic pipeline, a method combining ﬁnite element simulation and ﬂuid-structure in-
teraction vibration experiments is proposed for research. And the di ﬀerent pressures of 
the ﬂuid in the pipeline and the elastic modulus of the pipeline are considered variables, 
so complex systems can be calculated using this method. The biggest advantage of the 
present method is that the in ﬂuence of internal ﬂuid beats and external excitation can be 
comprehensively considered. It means that there is no need to replace methods and mod-
els when solving the working conditions of internal ﬂuid coupling and external excitation 
changes, in contrast to most existing methods. Thus, this method is simple, reduces meas-
urement time and cost, and has practical engineering value. 
Experimental measurements of a single pipeline in a simple laboratory apparatus are 
presented. The results calculated by the pr esented method are in good agreement with 
the experimental data, which veriﬁed the accuracy of the proposed method. 
The ﬂuid–structure interaction analysis and vibration characteristics of a cross-type 
hydraulic pipeline were carried out using the ﬁnite element method. As the ﬂuid pressure 
increases, the maximum deformation of the cross-type pipeline decreases in a small range, 
and the maximum stress shows a decreasing trend. But only lower-order modes are sig-
niﬁcantly aﬀected by modal analysis. As the elastic modulus of the pipeline increases, the 
maximum response value decreases, and the maximum stress shows a linear trend of in-
crease. While the higher-order modes are signi ﬁcantly aﬀected in modal analysis. With 
the increase in ﬂuid pressure and solid elastic modulus, the pipeline’s stiﬀness will go up. 
So the self-excited vibration period of the vibration system decreases, and the natural fre-
quency increases. 
Under external excitation, the ﬁrst, second, and ﬁfth-order modes of the cross-type 
tube are sensitive to external excitation forces. And the vibration direction is in the X di-

sign of liquid pipelines in practical engineering. When designing and installing the hy-
