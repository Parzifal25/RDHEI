ABSTRACT
Secure transmission of sensitive imagery requires techniques that embed auxiliary data within
encrypted content while guaranteeing exact recovery of both the payload and the original image.
Existingreversibledatahidinginencryptedimages(RDHEI)schemesrelyonhandcraftedpredictors
such as MED and GAP, which fail to capture complex spatial dependencies in natural images and
thereby restrict embedding capacity. We propose a lightweight convolutional neural network (CNN)
predictor, trained on natural image patches, that generates a per-pixel label map embedded directly
intothelowerbit-planesofAES-CTRencryptedimageswithoutauxiliaryside-channeltransmission.
On the USC SIPI benchmark, the proposed method reduces mean prediction error from 4.135 to
2.573 and achieves average embedding capacities of 5.158 bpp (grayscale) and 4.988 bpp (colour),
outperformingMED in10 of12 testcases. Allrecovered imagesattain PSNR=∞,SSIM= 1.0,and
MAE=0.0,confirminglosslessreversibility.Theseresultsdemonstratethatlearnedpredictionoffers
a principled improvement in embedding capacity for encrypted-domain reversible data hiding.
1. Introduction
The rapid growth of cloud-based image storage and
transmission has increased the need for methods that not
only protect image content but also allow additional infor-
mationtobeembeddedsecurely[30].Reversibledatahiding
(RDH) addresses this need by embedding a secret payload
into a cover image in such a way that the original image can
be perfectly reconstructed after extraction [3].
When the image is encrypted, the problem becomes
morechallenging.Thedatahidermustoperateentirelyinthe
cipher-domain without access to the original pixel values,
whilestillensuringthatboththehiddendataandtheoriginal
image can be recovered without any loss. This dual re-
quirementmakesreversibledatahidinginencryptedimages
(RDHEI) a technically demanding problem with important
applicationsinareassuchasmedicalimagestorage[1],mil-
itarycommunication,andprivacy-preservingcloudservices
[5].
EarlyRDHEImethodsprimarilyfollowedthereserving-
room-before-encryption (RRBE) paradigm (also referred to
asvacating-room-before-encryption),wherespaceiscreated
in the plaintext image prior to encryption to accommodate
embedded data. This framework was first introduced by
Zhang [8] and later improved by Hong et al. [7] through
more efficient space-reservation techniques. In parallel, the

Among the various approaches, prediction-based meth-
ods have become particularly effective. These methods es-
timate pixel values from their spatial neighbours, and the
resulting prediction error determines how much data can be
embedded.ClassicalpredictorssuchasMED[33],GAP,and
CALIC [32] make use of local image structures, but rely on
fixed heuristic rules. As a result, they are not well suited to
capture the diverse and complex patterns found in natural
images.
Predictionaccuracyisthereforeakeyfactorindetermin-
ingembeddingcapacity.Whenthepredictedvalueiscloseto
theactualpixelvalue,theresultingpredictionerrorissmall,
allowing more bit-planes to be used for embedding. Despite
significant progress in deep learning for image prediction,
most RDHEI methods continue to rely on handcrafted pre-
dictors, limiting their overall performance.
In this work, we address this limitation by introducing a
lightweight convolutional neural network (CNN) for pixel
prediction within the RRBE framework. The network is
trained on local image patches to learn spatial dependen-
cies directly from data, enabling more accurate predictions.
Based on these predictions, a per-pixel label map is con-
structed and embedded into the lower bit-planes of the
image prior to AES-CTR encryption, eliminating the need
for auxiliary side information. Experimental results on the
USC SIPI [35] benchmark demonstrate that the proposed
method improves prediction accuracy and increases embed-
ding capacity while maintaining perfect reversibility.

is integrated into a reversible data hiding framework based
on the RRBE architecture, where embedding is performed
on the original image prior to encryption. This contrasts
with RRAE-based schemes that operate directly on cipher-

5. Conclusion
The paper has presented a Lightweight CNN-based pre-
dictionmodelforreversibledatahidinginencryptedimages
inthecontextofRRBE.Thesuggestedalgorithm,inwhicha
learnedmodelsubstituteshuman-designedpredictors,offers
amoreeffectivemeansofrepresentingspatialdependencies
in natural pictures, resulting in greater prediction accuracy
and embedding capacity.
A small Lightweight CNN trained on local patches is
used to predict pixel values, and a per-pixel label map
is encoded into lower bit-planes and AES-CTR encrypted
without any auxiliary side-channel communication. Exper-
imental results on the USC SIPI [35] benchmark show a
34.6%reductioninpredictionerrorcomparedtoMED,with
average embedding capacities of 5.1584 bpp (grayscale)
and 4.9879 bpp (colour). All reconstructed images achieve
PSNR=∞,SSIM=1.0,andMAE=0.0,confirmingperfect
reversibility.
Futureworkmayexplorelargerreceptivefields,attention-
based architectures, and multi-channel prediction models to
furtherimproveperformanceoncomplextexturesandcolour
images. Adaptive label mapping strategies and integration
with advanced RDHEI frameworks also remain promising
directions.
